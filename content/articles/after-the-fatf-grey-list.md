## What happened
South Africa was placed on the FATF grey list of jurisdictions under increased monitoring in February 2023. The listing followed a 2021 mutual evaluation that found the country deficient in 20 of the 40 FATF recommendations, and South Africa agreed an action plan with 22 items. On 24 October 2025, after about 32 months, the FATF removed South Africa from the list, together with Nigeria, Mozambique and Burkina Faso.

## What delisting changes
Commentators, banks and business groups expect lower transaction costs for South African firms, more foreign investment and cheaper government borrowing. The rand firmed by nearly half a percent when the decision was announced. These are expectations and a first market reaction. Whether delisting produces a lasting fall in borrowing costs or a rise in investment can only be judged over time, using data on bond yields and capital flows.

## What delisting does not change
Exit does not end scrutiny. The FATF continues to monitor South Africa, and the country’s next FATF evaluation begins in 2026. Business for South Africa’s crime and corruption work stream says it is supporting efforts to keep the country off the list at that evaluation. The risk is no longer the listing itself but having to show again that the system works in practice: investigations, prosecutions, supervision and the use of financial intelligence.

## Questions for policymakers and compliance teams
1. What evidence of effectiveness (investigations, convictions, asset recovery and supervisory action) will South Africa present at the 2026 evaluation?
2. How will the institutions that delivered the action plan keep their capacity now that the pressure of the plan has ended?
3. How should banks and other accountable institutions maintain their compliance programmes rather than relax them?

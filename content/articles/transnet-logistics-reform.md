## Executive Summary
South Africa’s freight rail and container port networks—monopolized by state-owned logistics utility Transnet—have evolved into one of the country's primary macroeconomic supply-side bottlenecks. Rail tonnages delivered to the Richards Bay Coal Terminal and Saldanha iron ore terminals declined by over 30% from peak capacity, forfeiting tens of billions of Rands in mineral export windfalls and redirecting bulk freight onto national highways. This policy brief evaluates the implementation of the National Rail Policy and Freight Logistics Roadmap, focusing on third-party open access to the rail network, private-public partnerships (PPPs) at Durban Container Terminal Pier 2, and the regulatory challenges confronting the newly instituted Transport Economic Regulator (TER).

## Key Takeaways
1. **The Fiscus Opportunity Cost**: The Minerals Council South Africa estimated foregone export revenues exceeded R50 billion in 2023 alone due to locomotive shortages, cable theft, and maintenance backlogs.
2. **Third-Party Rail Access Implementation**: Establishing the Transnet Rail Infrastructure Manager (TRIM) to separate track infrastructure management from train operations represents the structural prerequisite for competitive private train operators to lease network slots.
3. **Port Terminal Concessioning**: Introducing international port operators (such as ICTSI at Durban Pier 2) is vital to upgrade ship turnaround times, improve crane moves per hour, and eliminate punitive shipping line congestion surcharges.
4. **Regulatory Oversight by TER**: The Economic Regulation of Transport Act provides the statutory mandate for the Transport Economic Regulator (TER) to adjudicate non-discriminatory slot allocation and transparent access tariffs.

## Strategic Imperatives
- Finalize cost-reflective, transparent track access tariff methodologies to incentivize private rolling-stock capital investment.
- Strengthen security enforcement on high-theft corridors (Mpumalanga coal line and Gauteng-eThekwini container corridor) through joint deployments with the South African Police Service (SAPS).
